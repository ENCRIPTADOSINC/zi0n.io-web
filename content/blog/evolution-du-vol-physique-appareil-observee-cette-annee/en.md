This year, stealing a smartphone is no longer just about selling used parts on the street. Thieves want what is kept inside: your banking apps, your crypto wallets, and your private messages. Once someone snatches your phone while it is unlocked, standard phone security simply is not enough.

## How phone thieves operate this year

Phone thefts have become fast, coordinated, and methodical. Criminal crews rely on a few well-practiced steps:

- **Peeking at your passcode (shoulder surfing):** In busy coffee shops, subway stations, or bars, thieves watch you enter your passcode or record your hands. Once they know your PIN, they plan the grab.
- **Drive-by snatches on scooters or bikes:** Attackers target people walking while looking down at directions or sending a text. They grab the phone while it is already unlocked and ride away, giving them instant access to your apps.
- **Signal-blocking pouches (Faraday bags):** Within seconds of taking your phone, thieves drop it into a metallic pouch that cuts off all wireless signals (cellular, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth). This prevents Find My or remote wipe commands from ever reaching the device.
- **Draining accounts in minutes:** Armed with the passcode they watched you type, thieves change your cloud passwords, turn off tracking, and transfer funds or cryptocurrency before you can even get to a computer.
- **Direct coercion:** In serious incidents, thieves directly threaten victims and demand that they unlock their phones on the spot.

## Everyday tips to stay safe

A few simple habits can make a huge difference in your security:

- **Use a longer passphrase:** A four-digit PIN is easy to memorize from afar. Switch to a longer password with letters and numbers.
- **Hide notifications on your lock screen:** Make sure text messages with two-factor authentication codes cannot be read without unlocking.
- **Set a short screen lock timer:** Make your screen turn off and lock after just a few seconds of inactivity.
- **Keep critical funds on a dedicated device:** Never store your life savings or main trading accounts on a standard phone you carry loosely in public.
