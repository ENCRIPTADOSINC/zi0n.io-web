## Critical threat vectors targeting active mobile traders

Traders routinely interact with sensitive API credentials, centralized exchange accounts, decentralized finance protocols, and non-custodial wallets. Adversaries exploit distinct attack surfaces inherent to mobile environments:

- **Screen espionage and clipboard hijacking:** Stealth malware monitors display buffers to log one-time passcodes, inspect wallet balances, and substitute withdrawal addresses during clipboard copy operations.
- **Targeted SIM swapping:** Cybercriminals leverage social engineering against cellular carriers to hijack phone numbers and intercept SMS verification codes for brokerage accounts.
- **IP tracking and distributed denial of service (DDoS):** Broadcasting your genuine IP address when connecting to RPC nodes or trading APIs allows competitors and hackers to pinpoint your location or disrupt connectivity during volatile market moves.
- **Forensic cable extraction (juice jacking):** Connecting a mobile device to public charging kiosks or compromised USB sockets exposes internal storage to automated extraction probes.
- **Physical coercion and robbery:** During conferences or international business travel, being pressured to unlock a terminal directly exposes all active portfolios and liquid funds.
